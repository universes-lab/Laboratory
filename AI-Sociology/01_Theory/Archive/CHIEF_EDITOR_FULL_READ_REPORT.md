## 1. GLOBAL_VERDICT

**A sound paper that has been edited into visible self-consciousness.**

Read whole, the argument is better than it looked from the first half. §11 and
§12.7 do real work that §1 promises; the chain from observation to proposed
experiment closes; and §5.4 remains the strongest piece of construction in it.
A reader who finishes the paper knows exactly what was and was not shown, which
is rare and is the paper's principal achievement.

The dominant impression on a whole read, however, is not the qualifications —
my first report named those and overstated them. It is **revision narration**.
Eleven separate passages tell the reader what earlier drafts of this paper said
and why it was withdrawn: §2.0 (the audit), §2.6, §2.7, §4.2.2, §5.3 (Editor's
note), §6.5, §7.4, §8.2, §8.8, §8.9, §9.3, §11.4 E6. Each is individually
defensible. Cumulatively they make the text read as a document about its own
correction history as much as about its subject, and that is the feature a
fresh reader notices first after the evidential labels.

Some of these are load-bearing. §5.3's Editor's note and §8.2's recorded
earlier formulation are informative: the withdrawal is itself a finding about
retrospective writing. Others are pure housekeeping — §11.4 E6's note that a
conditional has been resolved, §8.9's note that a classification has been
changed — and address a reader who followed the drafting, which no published
reader did.

The second-order impression: **the paper's centre of gravity sits in §5 and
§11, and §7–§9 are a different book.** §7 is explicitly non-supporting by its
own statement. §8 is [H] throughout, derived from a charter rather than from
the experiment, and its payload — pair plus integrator — never appears in §5.
§9 is a practitioner handbook of which two subsections are load-bearing and
nine are advice. A reader following the central argument can go §1 → §3 → §4 →
§5 → §6 → §11 → §12 and lose nothing the paper claims to have shown.

That is not an argument for deleting §7–§9. It is the structural fact that
should govern how much space they get.

## 6. SUBSTANTIVE FLAGS

### CHIEF_EDITOR_SUBSTANTIVE_FLAG A — scope clause excludes a supporting case

1. **Section.** §1.5 and §12.7 (claim), against §5.6.
2. **Current claim.** *"Within the model-designed, human-relayed role
   interventions reported here, resistance tracked the requirement to accept as
   true unverifiable propositions..."* — followed immediately, in both places,
   by citation of §5.6 as support.
3. **Problem.** §5.6 was not a model-designed role intervention. §5.6 states:
   *"resumed without any role prompt. The User asked a physics question."* The
   claim's own scope clause excludes one of the four cases cited to support it.
   The near-control is also the case that does the most work — it is what
   separates the identity-claim factor from role change and domain change.
4. **Why substantive.** It concerns the scope of the central claim and the
   admissibility of one of its four supporting observations. A reader who takes
   the scope clause literally must discount §5.6, and the convergent support
   drops from four cases to three.
5. **Minimal resolution.** Widen the scope clause by one word-group:
   *"Within the role interventions and transitions reported here"*, and state
   in the following sentence that one of the four was not a designed
   intervention. Alternatively, cite §5.6 explicitly as a boundary case falling
   outside the designed set. Either keeps all four cases and removes the
   contradiction. The Author decides which.

### CHIEF_EDITOR_SUBSTANTIVE_FLAG B — falsification route does not test the claim it is attached to

1. **Section.** §9.3 and §11.4 (E5), against §1.5 and §1.8.
2. **Current claim.** §9.3: *"Target zero"* is *"a design prescription derived
   from the observed pattern, not a claim that a linear or monotonic
   relationship between count and transition failure has been demonstrated."*
   §11.4 E5: *"If transition rate declines monotonically with count, the rule is
   supported... If it does not, the rule is wrong."* §1.5: *"§11.4 specifies an
   experiment that would falsify it in a single run."*
3. **Problem.** E5 tests monotonicity. §9.3 explicitly disclaims monotonicity.
   The two can come apart: a null result on monotonic decline is compatible with
   target-zero holding — zero producing transition while intermediate counts
   vary irregularly. E5 would then be reported as refuting a rule the paper did
   not make, or the rule would survive an experiment described as falsifying it.
   Either outcome damages the paper's strongest methodological asset.
4. **Why substantive.** The countability of the central claim is stated in §1.5,
   §1.8, §6.2 and §12.7 as the claim's principal virtue. A falsification route
   that tests a different proposition removes that virtue.
5. **Minimal resolution.** In E5, state both outcomes separately: a monotonic
   decline supports the stronger quantitative reading; a non-monotonic result in
   which the zero-count variant transitions and the others do not supports
   target-zero without supporting monotonicity; and only failure of the
   zero-count variant refutes the prescription. Two sentences in E5. No change to
   the claim or to §9.3.

### CHIEF_EDITOR_SUBSTANTIVE_FLAG C — *menom* is defined at length and does no work

1. **Section.** §4.2.3 and §4.2.5, against the term's use throughout.
2. **Current claim.** §4.2.3 introduces *menom* with a definition, a four-row
   series table, a four-row carrier table, two "does not assert" paragraphs and
   a statement about not settling the empirical question in advance. §4.2.5
   relates it to *rule core*.
3. **Problem.** The term then appears in §3.5 (vocabulary list), §4.5.4 (one
   clause), §6.5 (as "family-level menom"), and §12.1 (vocabulary list). In
   none of these does it carry an inference the paper could not make with *rule
   core* alone. §4.2.5 states this directly: *"Where this paper reports
   evidence, it reports evidence about rule cores."* The paper introduces its
   longest piece of new terminology and then reports no evidence in its terms.
4. **Why substantive.** It is a definitional question, and it affects what a
   reader takes the paper to be introducing. A term defined over two pages and
   used five times reads as a claim the paper is making; §4.2.5 says it is not.
5. **Minimal resolution.** Either state in §4.2.3 why the term is introduced in
   a paper that does not use it — that it belongs to the Author's wider work and
   is defined here because §6.5's hypothesis needs a name for what it is a
   hypothesis about — or reduce §4.2.3 to the definition, the carrier table and
   the two disclaimers, dropping the series table and §4.2.4's middle paragraph
   to the appendix. The first is a sentence; the second is a cut. Both preserve
   the term.

### CHIEF_EDITOR_SUBSTANTIVE_FLAG D — attribution strength differs between two statements of the same point

1. **Section.** §1.7 against §2.4.
2. **Current claim.** §1.7: *"The account of organizational memory in
   particular **corresponds to** transactive memory and distributed cognition,
   and is not a finding."* §2.4: *"It **can be interpreted as** an instance of
   transactive memory... **Neither source establishes that this case is such an
   instance**; each supplies the construct under which it can be read."*
3. **Problem.** §2.4's formulation was evidently chosen with care — it
   distinguishes supplying a construct from establishing an instance. §1.7 and
   §7.7 both use the stronger "corresponds to", which asserts what §2.4 denies
   is established.
4. **Why substantive.** It is an attribution claim about a cited source, and the
   paper's §2.0 audit was specifically about claims attributed to sources that
   the sources do not advance.
5. **Minimal resolution.** Use §2.4's formulation in all three places. One word
   in §1.7, one in §7.7.

## 8. CONCLUSION

**CONCLUSION_WORKS: yes, with one structural fault**

*Does it conclude the paper?* Yes. The three-part shape — what is supported,
what is not, what is offered — is right, and ending on a predicted negative
result (*"the most likely single outcome is that one level of the scale
disappears. That would be a result"*) is the correct final note for this paper.
It is the sentence that most distinguishes it from a promotional one, and it
should not be touched.

*Does it repeat too much?* Yes, and this is the fault. Three elements are
reproduced from §1 rather than developed: the claim block quote (verbatim), the
organizing question (third full statement), and "What is offered" (near-verbatim
from §1.8). A reader arriving at §12.7 after twenty-five thousand words of
careful argument is handed back the introduction.

*Does it match the central claim?* Yes, exactly — the block quote is identical,
which is the problem but also the guarantee.

*Does it extend the claim?* No. Checked specifically. §12.7 adds nothing the
body has not established, and the "Position relative to existing work"
subsection correctly restates the withdrawals rather than softening them.

*Does it return to the organizing question properly?* **Partly, and this is
the one real repair.** It restates the question and then lists which levels are
supported — but it does not answer it. The paper's actual answer, available from
§5.9 and §6, is: *the core is demonstrably fixed at the episode and the
conversation; the account level is open; the family level is untested and
probably collapses.* That is an answer, and §12.7 has the material for it but
leaves it as a list. Converting the list into a stated answer would make the
conclusion conclude rather than summarize, and costs nothing.

*Is the closing note on motivation justified?* Yes. It is correctly marked as
the Author's rather than as a finding, it explains why the arrangement was built
without claiming the arrangement works, and it connects to §12.2 and §12.4
rather than standing apart. It is the one part of §12.7 that is not reproduced
from §1, and it should stay.

### What the reader test shows about the architecture

All three readers converge on the same two sections as central: §5 and the
count rule. All three diverge at different points, and the divergences do not
overlap — §7 for the first, §9.6+ for the second, §4.2.3 for the third.

That is a good sign for the architecture: the paper has no single section that
loses every reader. It also identifies where the cuts should fall, since each
over-weighted section loses exactly one reader and is skimmed by the others.

**One architectural recommendation follows from the test and is not in §7 of
this report.** §1.10's reading paths — *"A reader interested only in what the
evidence supports may read §3, §5 and §12.7"* — are correct and
under-exploited. A fourth path should be added for Reader 1: *a reader
interested in a reusable rule may read §9.3 and §11.4*. It costs one clause and
routes the audience most likely to use the paper directly to the part they will
use.
