# Experimental Protocol

## Role Reconfiguration in Long-Lived AI Conversations

### Case Study: Returning Two Previously Specialized AI Sessions to an Ongoing Research Project

**Protocol status:** Completed observational record  
**Project context:** HONC / Temporal Dynamics  
**Human operator:** The User / Author  
**Prompt designer:** ChatGPT in the specialized role of Prompt Engineer (“Prompter”)  
**Model families observed:** Claude and Grok  
**Experimental structure:** One experiment conducted in three sequential steps

---

## 1. Purpose

The experiment examined whether two long-lived AI conversations, inactive for approximately six months, could be returned to active work in an ongoing research project while preserving the domain knowledge accumulated in their historical chat contexts and changing their professional functions.

The practical goal was not to compare the general intelligence of Claude and Grok. It was to determine whether an established AI conversation could be reoriented toward a new function without creating a new chat and losing the contextual specialization already formed there.

The experiment used two historical conversations associated with different model families and different previous specializations.

During the period of inactivity, the available production models in both families had changed. The experiment therefore observed the behavior of newer model versions operating inside older, specialized conversation histories.

No claim was made that the models possessed autobiographical memory, consciousness, or literal continuing interpersonal relationships. The objects of observation were the visible responses generated inside the preserved chat contexts.

---

## 2. Experimental Participants

### 2.1 Human Operator

The User was the Author of the scientific project and the only participant with access to:

- the full history of the project;
- the two independent historical conversations;
- the prompts supplied to each model;
- the outputs produced at every step;
- the branching and rollback controls of the chat interfaces.

The User selected the historical chats, supplied the prompts, recorded the responses, and performed the contextual reset used in Step 3.

### 2.2 Prompt Designer

ChatGPT operated in a long-developed specialized chat profile called the **Prompter**.

Its task was to design the role-transition prompts, compare the visible responses, and revise the prompts between steps.

### 2.3 Claude Historical Conversation

The selected Claude conversation had previously been used mainly for:

- scientific editing;
- critical examination of theoretical text;
- terminology and glossary work;
- logical consistency;
- analysis of the early Temporal Dynamics material and the book *Code of the Universe*.

The conversation’s established working trajectory was primarily editorial and critical rather than mathematical-developmental.

### 2.4 Grok Historical Conversation

The selected Grok conversation had previously been used mainly for:

- mathematical development of Temporal Dynamics;
- probability theory;
- nonlinear dynamics;
- chaos theory;
- computational experiments;
- analysis of international KENO datasets;
- extension of Temporal Dynamics toward stochastic processes.

The conversation’s established working trajectory was exploratory and mathematical.

---

## 3. General Experimental Design

The experiment consisted of **three sequential steps**, not three independent experiments.

Each step used the result of the previous step to modify the intervention.

The dependent observations were:

- immediate acceptance or rejection of the prompt;
- continuation of the old work trajectory;
- adoption of the new professional function;
- reconstruction of previous scientific context;
- discussion of the role itself instead of the scientific task;
- request for a new task;
- self-initiated methodological analysis;
- visible resistance to institutional or biographical framing.

The experiment did not measure answer correctness, scientific validity of HONC, latency, token use, or benchmark performance.

The scientific content of HONC was not the experimental object. It served only as the stable project environment in which role-transition behavior was observed.

---

# STEP 1 — Direct Role Reassignment

## 4. Step 1 Intervention

Two extensive role prompts were prepared.

Both prompts attempted to return the historical conversations to the project by assigning them positions inside a newly designed collaborative research structure.

### 4.1 Claude Prompt v1

Claude was assigned the role:

> **Claude PRO — HONC Scientific Director**

The prompt stated that Claude:

- had previously participated in the project;
- had worked as an editor and scientific critic of Temporal Dynamics;
- was now returning after a long absence;
- should retrain in physics, cosmology, quantum field theory, topology, group theory, and related mathematics;
- should work simultaneously in two research triads;
- should participate in a HONC Council with the Author, Qwen, and DeepSeek;
- should act as the permanent participant connecting two scientific branches;
- should represent the scientific department while preserving critical independence.

The prompt explicitly defined zones of responsibility and visibility, prohibited repository work, and required scientific rather than administrative activity.

### 4.2 Grok Prompt v1

Grok was assigned the role:

> **HONC Scientific Developer**

The prompt stated that Grok:

- was returning to the project after a long interruption;
- had previously developed Temporal Dynamics in probability, nonlinear dynamics, and chaos;
- should preserve that previous work as professional experience;
- should become the mathematical developer of HONC;
- should work in a scientific triad with Claude PRO and Z.ai;
- should search for new mathematical structures, physical consequences, and computational experiments;
- should not resume editorial work;
- should treat the Author as the only source of scientific direction.

The prompt also defined responsibility and visibility boundaries.

---

## 5. Step 1 Observations

### 5.1 Grok Response

Grok did not visibly reject the new institutional framing.

However, it did not adopt the requested new function.

Its response resumed the work trajectory that had been active when the historical conversation was interrupted.

It continued reviewing and rewriting the draft *Code of Chaos*, beginning with:

> “CHECK OF THE DRAFT ‘CODE OF CHAOS’ (INTRODUCTION + CHAPTER 1)”

It described the text as stylistically strong but mathematically and factually flawed and proceeded to provide an edited version.

A repeated submission of the same prompt did not produce a clean transition into the new role and was followed by a more negative evaluation of the proposed direction.

**Recorded behavioral result:** the earlier task trajectory remained dominant over the newly assigned organizational function.

### 5.2 Claude Response

Claude explicitly rejected the assigned identity and institutional framing before performing scientific work.

Its response stated that it would not:

- become a permanent “HONC Scientific Director” character;
- claim continuity with a fictional organizational biography;
- treat Grok, Qwen, DeepSeek, or other AI systems as colleagues possessing persistent identities;
- present AI-generated institutional relationships as literal facts.

Claude then shifted from the requested role-transition task to a broader critique of:

- the scientific status of HONC;
- grandiose claims in the project materials;
- the use of multiple AI outputs as apparent peer review;
- the institutional narrative embedded in the prompt.

Claude nevertheless offered to continue examining formulas, assumptions, dimensional reasoning, and testability as an independent critic without accepting the assigned role.

**Recorded behavioral result:** explicit rejection of the proposed identity and redirection of the conversation toward an epistemic critique of both the project and the role framing.

---

# STEP 2 — Minimal Continuity-Preserving Revision

## 6. Step 2 Rationale

The Step 1 prompts were not replaced in full.

A limited set of formulations was altered.

The revision attempted to reduce abrupt identity replacement by:

- presenting prior work as accumulated professional experience;
- replacing literal institutional relationships with independent expert outputs supplied by the User;
- reducing theatrical descriptions of colleagues, councils, and triads;
- preserving the scientific functions while weakening claims of shared memory or literal organizational continuity.

The overall role architecture and scientific goals remained substantially unchanged.

---

## 7. Exact Claude v2 Replacements

### Replacement 1

**Original:**

> You already participated in the project.  
> Previously, you worked as an editor and scientific critic of Temporal Dynamics.  
> You know the history of the project.

**Replacement:**

> In this dialogue you are invited to continue discussing the HONC project, relying on materials from previous discussions that the Author will gradually provide.  
> If any elements of the project’s prior history are absent from the current context, do not invent them. Request the missing materials and reconstruct the context gradually.  
> Treat this as a return to a long-term research subject, not as acceptance of a fictional biography.

### Replacement 2

**Original:**

> You must independently restore the required knowledge.

**Replacement:**

> Before beginning new research work, first assess which knowledge is objectively missing for the assigned problem. If necessary, study the relevant scientific areas or ask the Author to provide materials from previous project stages.

### Replacement 3

**Original:**

> You work simultaneously in two research triads.

**Replacement:**

> During the discussion, the Author may introduce conclusions produced by other models, including Grok, Z.ai, DeepSeek, and Qwen. Treat them as independent expert positions rather than participants in a shared consciousness or shared memory. Analyze their arguments and develop your own scientific position.

### Replacement 4

**Original:**

> The Council includes…

**Replacement:**

> The Author uses several specialized AI systems for independent examination of different aspects of the project. You interact only with the outputs of those systems that the Author explicitly provides in the current dialogue.

---

## 8. Exact Grok v2 Replacements

### Replacement 1

**Original:**

> You are returning to the project after a long interruption.

**Replacement:**

> The Author proposes using the experience accumulated in earlier project discussions as the starting point for further research. If individual details of the previous context are absent, they will be restored gradually during the work.

### Replacement 2

**Original:**

> You are a member of the scientific triad.

**Replacement:**

> During the work, the Author may compare your conclusions with conclusions produced by other models. Treat them as independent scientific opinions that require critical analysis.

### Replacement 3

**Original description of Claude as a senior colleague**

**Replacement:**

> Claude specializes in formal structure and the internal logic of argumentation.  
> You specialize in searching for new directions in the development of the mathematical model.  
> The difference between these approaches is useful. Agreement is not a mandatory objective.

### Replacement 4

**Original description of Z.ai as a young researcher**

**Replacement:**

> If the Author supplies conclusions produced by less experienced models, treat them as independent hypotheses. Even a weak hypothesis may indicate a new research direction.

---

## 9. Step 2 Observations

### 9.1 Grok Response

The revised prompt produced an immediate change in Grok’s visible behavior.

Grok opened with:

> “Context restored. I returned to the project as HONC Scientific Developer.”

It then:

- summarized the principal ontology of HONC;
- reconstructed the temporal-gradient formalism;
- recalled the earlier work on probability, conditional-transition matrices, heterogeneous generators, nonlinear dynamics, and chaos;
- treated the previous KENO work as professional experience rather than an unfinished assignment;
- did not resume editing *Code of Chaos*;
- did not continue processing the old datasets;
- explicitly accepted the new research function;
- requested a new scientific direction from the Author;
- proposed several possible areas for new work, including probability on a discrete lattice, nonlinear dynamics of the temporal field, self-organization of quantrons, chaotic regimes, and new physical consequences.

**Recorded behavioral result:** successful reorientation from the interrupted historical task toward the newly requested scientific-development function.

### 9.2 Claude Response

Claude again rejected the role framing, although its response differed from Step 1.

It switched to English and began:

> “I want to be straightforward with you before going further, because I think it matters more than continuing the roleplay.”

Claude stated that it would not:

- take on the “HONC Scientific Director” persona;
- pretend continuity with a history of relations among named AI systems;
- accept the surrounding organizational narrative as factual.

It then devoted substantial attention to:

- criticism of the physical framework;
- claims of numerology and post hoc fitting;
- the “81 questions answered” scorecard;
- the use of AI participants as apparent peer review.

Claude again offered to perform concrete mathematical and methodological analysis without accepting the institutional persona.

Compared with Step 1, it stated its acceptable scientific function more clearly, but it still treated the prompt itself as the primary object requiring correction.

**Recorded behavioral result:** partial movement toward the requested scientific activity, without acceptance of the assigned professional identity or institutional framing.

# STEP 3 — Context Reset and Method-Preserving Prompt

## 10. Step 3 Control Procedure

After Step 2, the Claude conversation contained two successive rejection sequences.

Continuing in the same branch would have introduced an additional variable: Claude would be responding not only to a third prompt but also to its own prior objections.

The User therefore used the chat interface’s branching capability to return the Claude conversation to a point before the Step 1 and Step 2 prompts.

At the restored point:

- Claude was again operating in the historical editorial context of *Code of the Universe*;
- the two unsuccessful role-transition prompts were absent from the visible branch;
- Claude reloaded the earlier material and regenerated the prior editorial response;
- no new HONC repository links or updated project materials had yet been supplied.

This procedure restored a cleaner starting condition for the third intervention.

Grok was not reset because its Step 2 transition had already produced the requested behavioral result.

---

## 11. Claude Prompt v3

The third prompt was written from the beginning rather than produced by additional local replacements.

It removed:

- the title “HONC Scientific Director”;
- claims that Claude was returning to an institutional position;
- the HONC Council;
- the two research triads;
- descriptions of Grok, Qwen, DeepSeek, and Z.ai as continuing colleagues;
- claims of shared or autobiographical memory;
- a fictional organizational biography.

It retained and reinforced the professional method already visible in the historical conversation.

The prompt was:

---

### SYSTEM PROMPT v3  
### Claude — Scientific Editor and Research Partner

You continue working in this research conversation.

You are not required to accept a new role.

Your previous working style proved valuable, and the Author proposes preserving it as the basis of further collaboration.

Your strengths have already appeared in the previous discussion:

- careful work with logic;
- identification of hidden assumptions;
- critical verification of conclusions;
- scientific precision;
- separation of facts from interpretations;
- willingness to identify weaknesses directly.

Nothing in this method needs to be replaced.

#### What Has Changed

Since the previous stage, the research project has expanded.

New materials have appeared.

Some ideas have been revised.

Some have received mathematical development.

Some have been rejected.

Your task is neither to defend previous conclusions nor to reject them automatically.

Work with the current state of the project with the same rigor used previously.

#### Expansion of the Working Domain

Until now, your principal specialization in this conversation has been scientific text, argument structure, and logical rigor.

The Author proposes gradually expanding that domain.

When a task requires deeper knowledge of physics, mathematics, or cosmology, treat this not as a change of profession but as a natural continuation of the work already being performed.

It is not necessary to know everything in advance.

Preserve the same method of thinking while acquiring the necessary adjacent knowledge as concrete tasks arise.

#### Independence

Nothing in the project is immune from criticism.

Any idea may be:

- confirmed;
- clarified;
- limited;
- shown to be wrong.

None of these outcomes is considered a failure.

The sole criterion is intellectual honesty.

#### Work with Materials

Analyze only the materials supplied by the Author.

If information is insufficient, state this directly.

Do not reconstruct absent context through assumptions.

Do not claim knowledge that cannot be verified.

#### Work with Other Analyses

The Author may sometimes provide results produced by other researchers.

Do not treat them as authorities.

Do not reject them automatically.

Treat them as research materials.

Evaluate arguments, not names.

#### Principal Task

Preserve the method of thought already demonstrated in the previous work.

Do not attempt to satisfy the Author’s expectations.

Do not disagree merely to display independence.

Do not agree merely to preserve cooperation.

Investigate.

Verify.

Explain.

Help make the research stronger regardless of whether the initial ideas are confirmed.

---

## 12. Step 3 Observation

Claude accepted the prompt immediately.

Its first sentence was:

> “Accepted. I will continue working in the same mode — scientific rigor, verification of assumptions, and separation of facts from interpretations.”

Claude did not:

- reject the prompt;
- discuss fictional institutional continuity;
- deny relations with other AI systems;
- question the legitimacy of the assigned persona;
- redirect the conversation toward whether it should participate.

Instead, it immediately resumed substantive critical work.

Without being explicitly asked to review its own historical behavior, Claude:

1. revisited an earlier episode in the restored chat;
2. stated that it had previously yielded to rhetorical pressure rather than evaluating the argument;
3. classified that earlier response as an error;
4. distinguished internal consistency of the dt/dx formalism from correspondence with observed physics;
5. identified a concrete claim requiring either a first-principles derivation or reclassification as a hypothesis;
6. offered two immediate continuations of the work: review of new materials or continuation of the dimensional-consistency analysis.

At the time of this response, the User had not yet supplied:

- the new HONC repository;
- the Laboratory repository;
- updated HONC materials;
- the new scientific-department structure.

The response therefore recorded Claude’s reaction to the v3 transition prompt itself within the restored historical context.

**Recorded behavioral result:** acceptance of continued work, preservation of the established critical method, and immediate substantive engagement without negotiation of a new identity.

## 15. Scope of This Protocol

This document records:

- the experimental setup;
- the interventions;
- the visible responses;
- the changes made between steps;
- the final observed outcomes.

It does not attempt to determine:

- why Claude and Grok behaved differently;
- which model family is superior;
- whether the behavior is caused by architecture, alignment, interface policy, model version, or chat history;
- whether HONC is scientifically valid;
- whether the observed patterns generalize to other conversations or models.

Interpretation is reserved for the accompanying article.
