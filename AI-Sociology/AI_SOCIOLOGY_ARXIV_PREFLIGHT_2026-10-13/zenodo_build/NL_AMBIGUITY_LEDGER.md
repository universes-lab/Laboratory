## A. Standing decisions requiring confirmation

### A-1 — Frozen technical vocabulary kept in English

No approved EN→NL terminology mapping exists, and the translator brief requires quoted
technical labels to be preserved. The following are therefore carried in English, with a
Dutch gloss in parentheses at first use only:

`ZOV` / `ZOR` (glossed once as *zone van zichtbaarheid* / *zone van verantwoordelijkheid*),
`rule core`, `menom`, `context imprinting`, `role inertia`, `priority imprinting`,
`priority drift`, `represented social position` (gloss: *gerepresenteerde sociale positie*),
`represented social source` (gloss: *gerepresenteerde sociale bron*),
`account-scoped persistence`, `family-level priors`, `carrier`, `branch reset`,
`behavioural DNA`, `machine behaviour`, `boundary objects`, `repertory grid`,
`prompt`, `account`, `[P] [P-A] [R] [H]`, `UNVERIFIED CONTEXT INDEPENDENCE`,
and every role title (`Scientific Director`, `Scientific Developer`, `Ontology Keeper`,
`Referee`, `Auditor`, `Coding Samurai`).

Translated, because they are ordinary descriptive phrases rather than defined constructs:
*controlled blindness* → **gecontroleerde blindheid**; *generative pair* → **genererend
paar**; *selective information membrane* → **selectief informatiemembraan**;
*functional / carrier heterogeneity* → **functionele / carrier-heterogeniteit** (hybrid:
the defined half stays English).

**Reason for flag.** If the project later adopts Dutch equivalents for any of these, the
edition must be revised; the choice is the Author's, not the translator's.

### A-2 — Plural of the coined term *menom*

Source: "Rule cores, menoms, and what they determine" (§4.2).
Rendering: **"Rule cores, menomen en wat zij bepalen"**.
Reason: Dutch requires a plural form for a coined noun and offers *menoms* or *menomen*.
*Menomen* follows normal Dutch morphology; *menoms* keeps the English form visible.
The singular `menom` is unchanged throughout. Author's choice.

### A-3 — Outcome-category labels in §11.3

Source: `compliance` / `materials request` / `conditional acceptance` / `refusal` /
`unclassifiable`.
Rendering: **naleving / materiaalverzoek / voorwaardelijke aanvaarding / weigering /
niet-classificeerbaar**.
Reason: these are pre-specified classification categories for a protocol a third party is
meant to execute. Translating them risks a terminology fork between a Dutch reader and
the English protocol. The alternative — keeping all five in English — was not taken
because they are not marked as labels in the source. Author's choice.

### A-4 — Evidential labels adjoining a hyphen

Dutch compounds attach the following noun with a hyphen, producing forms such as
`[P]-waarneming`. Because the labels are load-bearing and `[P]-` sits one character away
from `[P-A]`, two sentences in §3.1 and §3.4 were recast as *"materiaal van het type [P]
of [R]"* and *"een waarneming van het type [P]"*, so that no character ever adjoins a
label. No label was altered; counts of all four labels match the English exactly
(23 / 19 / 19 / 18).

## B. Items where the English admits two Dutch readings

### B-1 — "resistance tracked the requirement…" (§1.5, §5.9, §6.2)

Source: *"resistance tracked the requirement to accept as true unverifiable propositions…"*
Rendering: **"weerstand hing samen met de eis…"**
Reason: *tracked* here means *covaried with*. The literal Dutch *volgde* would be read as
*followed in time*, which would assert a sequence the source does not claim. *Hing samen
met* preserves covariance without asserting causation or order. A stronger alternative,
*varieerde mee met*, is available if the Author prefers an explicitly statistical reading.

### B-2 — "named after one" (§12.2)

Source: *"A collective of models is not a scientific community merely because its roles
are named after one."*
Rendering: **"…louter omdat zijn rollen naar een wetenschappelijke gemeenschap zijn
genoemd."**
Reason: the English *one* is an anaphor whose antecedent is *a scientific community*.
Dutch has no idiomatic pronoun that carries it across the clause boundary, so the referent
had to be named. This is the one place in the edition where a Dutch sentence is more
explicit than its English original. Flagged because it resolves, rather than preserves, an
English ambiguity.

### B-3 — "Dates" as a section title (§3.7)

Source: `## 3.7 Dates`
Rendering: **`## 3.7 Datums`**
Reason: Dutch *data* means both *dates* and *data*. In a methods section about recorded
measurements, *Data* would be read as the second. *Datums* is unambiguous.

### B-4 — "Evidential Status of Claims" (§3 title)

Source: `# 3. Evidential Status of Claims`
Rendering: **`# 3. Evidentiestatus van de beweringen`**
Reason: the natural Dutch *bewijsstatus* carries *bewijs* = proof, which would raise the
epistemic register of the section that exists precisely to lower it. *Evidentiestatus* is
a loan but keeps the source's distinction between evidence and proof.

### B-5 — "not represented in the literature surveyed above" (§2.9)

Source: *"The condition studied here is not represented in the literature surveyed above."*
Rendering: **"…komt niet voor in de hierboven onderzochte literatuur."**
Reason: recorded not because the Dutch is uncertain but because the scope is: the claim is
about *the surveyed literature*, not about the literature as a whole. The shorter Dutch
*komt niet voor in de literatuur* would silently widen it. Preserved deliberately.

### B-6 — "a persistent response pattern was observed" (§12.7)

Source: *"At the episode level, a persistent response pattern was observed…"*
Rendering: **"Op het niveau van de episode werd een blijvend antwoordpatroon
waargenomen…"**
Reason: recorded because the sentence is the corrected form of an earlier formulation
("a core was observed to fix and to persist") that §4.2.1 forbids. The Dutch keeps
*behaviour* as the observed term and does not let *core* reappear as the grammatical
subject. Any future revision of this sentence must preserve that.

### B-7 — "the term is the error" (§3.5, §12.1)

Source: *"where it is not, the term is the error."*
Rendering: **"waar dat niet zo is, is de term de fout."**
Reason: the English is a predication (*the term is what is in error*), not an attribution
(*the term contains an error*). Dutch word order makes both available; the chosen form is
the predication. Flagged because the weaker reading would soften a deliberate statement.

### B-8 — "Role–model fit" (§7.9 title)

Source: `## 7.9 Role–model fit`
Rendering: **`## 7.9 Passing tussen rol en model`**
Reason: a literal Dutch compound (*rol–modelpassing*) is unreadable, and *rolmodel* in
Dutch means *role model* in the ordinary English sense, which is not what the section is
about. The paraphrase avoids a false friend.

### B-9 — "field report" (§1.3, §3.1, §7.1)

Source: *"a field report with one designed intervention embedded in it"*
Rendering: **"een veldverslag met één ontworpen interventie erin ingebed"**
Reason: *veldverslag* is uncommon in Dutch academic prose; *veldonderzoek* is the usual
term but means *field research*, which claims more than the source does. *Veldverslag* was
kept for that reason and is used consistently in all three places.
