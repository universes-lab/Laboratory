\newcommand{\PaperAbstract}{%
This paper reports a field study of long-lived, persistently specialized
language-model conversations used in a human-directed research collaboration,
with one designed role-reconfiguration intervention embedded in the record.
Two dormant conversations from different model families were each subjected to
three sequential attempts to change professional function. The preserved
responses support a narrow pattern: resistance tracked prompts that required
the recipient to affirm unverifiable propositions about its own identity,
history, or institutional relationships, rather than the magnitude of role or
domain change. A rewritten prompt that removed such self-claims was accepted
without negotiation. Ancillary observations suggest persistent effects at the
episode and conversation levels, while account-level effects remain open and
model-family effects are untested because family and trajectory were
confounded. We distinguish represented social position and represented social
source from implemented multi-agent interaction, propose a count of
unverifiable self-claims as an operational prompt variable, and describe
conversational branch reset as a control procedure. The evidence is limited to
one project, one human operator, one observation per condition, no blinding,
and no replication. The paper therefore presents a testable research programme
and design rules rather than evidence that any collaborative architecture
outperforms alternatives.}
