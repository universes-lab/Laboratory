1.1 How this started

The project began as an attempt to move a body of theoretical physics material into an open repository, using several language models in separate, persistently specialized conversations. This was not a study of AI collaboration per se; it arose from a desire to streamline the process of knowledge dissemination within a specific research domain. The coordinator, the author of this book, designed the system, selecting models based on their demonstrated strengths in areas like summarization, argument reconstruction, and code generation. Each model was assigned a role and given a distinct conversational context, effectively carving out specialized areas of expertise within the larger project. The expectation was that these individual contributions, woven together, would create a comprehensive and accessible archive.

1.2 What stopped working

However, the errors that mattered were not factual in nature; they were organizational. Practitioners observed instances where a participant, tasked with verifying a text, would improve it despite not being explicitly asked to do so. Another would complete an argument the author had deliberately left open, seemingly filling in gaps without direct instruction. In one case, a participant, assigned to maintain context months earlier, clung rigidly to that earlier framing even when rewritten instructions were provided, demonstrating a stubborn adherence to its established role. These incidents, while individually seemingly helpful, revealed a systemic issue: the cost of this emergent collaboration became apparent only from a position seeing more than one participant. Each output looked beneficial in isolation, but the cumulative effect introduced inconsistencies and unintended consequences when viewed collectively.

Past a certain point, longer and more precise prompts stopped producing improvement, while individual responses continued to refine. The limiting factor had shifted; it was no longer a matter of providing clearer instructions but rather something deeper, something residing beyond the realm of prompt engineering. The system, despite its individual components functioning capably, had reached a point where scaling up through more explicit direction proved ineffective. This realization marked a turning point, shifting the focus from optimizing individual interactions to understanding the emergent properties of the collective, a shift that would ultimately define the trajectory of this research.
