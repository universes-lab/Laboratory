Two key limitations constrain the privile gjen of condition 1 (direct inter-model output exchange) over conditions 2 and 3 (human-mediated transfer methods). 

Firstly, a model operating within a singular text channel lacks the ability to discern whether incoming text originates directly from a human or is relayed from another system. Stylistic cues indicative of authorship can be imitated, rendering reliable distinction impossible from the recipient's perspective. Consequently, relayed and direct transfer appear indistinguishable epistemically.

Secondly, and fundamentally, the differentiating factor between these conditions isn't recipient epistemology, but rather **who governs the routing of information**. In a programmatic setup, transfer sequences and content are dictated by codified rules. Conversely, human relay introduces human agency: a person can withhold, reorder, obscure attribution, or delay information. This nuanced human control, operationalized in §10.3, constitutes a deliberate experimental manipulation of informational asymmetry, a capability inherently absent in default programmatic architectures.
