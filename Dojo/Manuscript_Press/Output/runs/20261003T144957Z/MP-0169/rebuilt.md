The earlier conclusion that codified role precedence over explicit phrasing in programmatic instructions requires qualification. While the redefined function demonstrably mitigated unauthorized modifications, the shift in behavior stemmed not from altering the instruction list itself, but from restructuring the operative AGENT TYPE as redefined within the prompt itself. This distinction aligns with the core question §5 aimed to address: the delineation between role-based influence and direct instruction modification.

A more precise articulation of the operative structural unit originates from the project's collaboration charter, predating the present authors' subsequent formulations. This charter posits that the fundamental generative unit is **a generative pair plus a non-participating integrator (as adopted in §8)**. Specifically, it delineates a minimal dyad for generation (one agent for monologue, two for dialogue), posits detrimental output beyond that dyad without strict coordination, and assigns a distinct role to a third participant: maintaining the task frame, resolving divergences, and integrating conclusions, rather than direct argumentation, solution generation, or dialogue substitution. This framework provides a clearer definition than theTagMode CONCEPT of three debating agents employed subsequently.
