Two key reasons compel us to publish at this stage. Firstly, the experiment's instructive failures shed crucial light on the underlying dynamics. The infatti that the initial two interventions faltered provided invaluable insight precisely because they did not succeed as expected. Had only the successful third step been observed, a premature and unsupported conclusion of method-preserving prompt efficacy would have been drawn, obscuring the necessary understanding of *why* and the presence of unconcluded confounds (§11.8). 

Secondly, replicating these specific conditions in a controlled laboratory setting proves inherently difficult. The inherently dynamic and specialized nature of dormant, months-old conversations within a live research project makes for a context uniquely suited for observational learning rather than tightly controlled inference. While not ideal for deriving definitive causal conclusions, this environment uniquely enabled the identification of critical phenomena and the formulation of testable hypotheses for future work.
