The vocabulary introduced thus far, conceptualized within the framework of human cultural and behavioral transmission, employs terms like the "collective unconscious." However, it's crucial to explicitly state that this paper neither asserts nor presupposes the existence of such structures in humans nor attributes a defined physical carrier to them. 

The explicit focus shifts to artificial systems. These systems instantiate informational and behavioral structures analogous to those described in the human context, and crucially, these instantiations within artificial systems are subject to direct or indirect examination through empirically verifiable means like examining training corpora, weights, context states, and interaction protocols. **The parallel drawn to human informational structures does not imply their confirmation in humans. Rather, it highlights the conceptual framework's applicability to artificial systems while leaving the nature of human informational structures an open question explicitly unanswered within this discourse.**
