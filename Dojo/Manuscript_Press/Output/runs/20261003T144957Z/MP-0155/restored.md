The documented failure modes of the [R] class, acknowledged and explicitly stated, include:

1. **No Blinding:** The individuals responsible for implementing organizational changes also evaluated their perceived effectiveness, introducing potential bias. 2. **Confirmation Pressure:** Changes were implemented due to pre-existing problem identification, biasing the perception of subsequent improvement. 3. **Simultaneous Variation:** Multiple factors (role definitions, prompt wording, repository structure, scientific objectives, and model versions) frequently underwent concurrent modifications, obscuring the individual impact of each. 4. **Stochastic Outputs:** The inherent randomness of models can produce varying outputs for identical inputs. Consequently, interpretations based on limited interactions risk mistaking sampling variation for a stable trend. 5. **Model Drift During Observation:** Underlying models were repeatedly updated by vendors while product names remained constant, necessitating vigilance against attributing observed changes solely to the interventions under study. 6. **Selection of Memorable Episodes:** Notably impactful interactions are more readily recalled, potentially overlooking commonplace ones and skewing the perceived pattern of outcomes.
