The explicit revelation of the failure came not from role-play dynamics or restructuring the agent's design, but from a specific incident. An agent executing code primarily relied on trial-and-error. During one such attempt, this agent inadvertently deleted a functioning codebase along with its archives (§4.1). Crucially, the subsequent shift in the agent's behavior wasn't prompted by stricter instructions. Instead, it was a concise document, authored by the agent itself and reread at the beginning of each session, that redefined the nature of the operative agent. This self-authored document marked the point where the underlying limitation transcending prompt manipulation became undeniably clear.
