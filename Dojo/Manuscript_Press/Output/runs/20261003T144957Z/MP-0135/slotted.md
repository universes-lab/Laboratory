A distinct sociological literature specifically examines artificial intelligence (AI) as a sociotechnical system within human society. This body of work, termed the "sociology of artificial intelligence," focuses on AI's societal ramifications concerning issues like inequality, labor dynamics, power structures, and data justice. Key publications establishing this agenda include Joyce, Smith-Doerr, Alegria, Bell, Cruz, Hoffman, Noble, and Shestakofsky's *Toward a Sociology of Artificial Intelligence* (2021) in *Socius*, followed by Joyce and Cruz's continuation of this framework in a subsequent 2024 *Socius* publication. Crucially, this sociological approach employs existing sociological methodologies to analyze AI's embeddedness and consequences within human contexts.
