The paramount finding highlighted by Zhang et al. is that model heterogeneity consistently enhances the performance of debate frameworks otherwise exhibiting subpar results. Crucially, this heterogeneity pertains to the distinct models underlying the agents, not to the functions those agents embody. This insight onderscores a distinction the literature often overlooks, a distinction explicitly developed in §8.9: heterogeneity of the *carrier* (the specific model implementing a role) is conceptually distinct from heterogeneity of the agent's *function* (the role's responsibilities and permissible information access). Zhang et al.'s work directly addresses carrier heterogeneity, implicitly licensing the independent investigation of function heterogeneity, a dimension this paper subsequently explores.
