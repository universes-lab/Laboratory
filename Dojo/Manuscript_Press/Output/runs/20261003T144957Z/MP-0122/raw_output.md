Shanahan, McDonell, and Reynolds propose a theoretical framework for understanding language models through the lens of role-play. Instead of viewing a model as possessing a singular, persistent identity, they argue it simulates a distribution of potential characters. This framework posits that prompts act as situational cues, eliciting corresponding behavioral and epistemic stances akin to actors embodying different roles. Consequently, a prompt requesting institutional biography, lacking a coherent biographical referent within the model's simulated framework, would result in the model attempting to construct a fictional persona consistent with the institutional aspetct of the prompt. Section §5.3 empirically exemplifies this, demonstrating the model's response to such a prompt. Furthermore, the operational finding articulated in §6.2 can be interpreted as a quantifiable measure of how effectively a prompt directs the model towards embodying a specific, constructed role, thus illustrating the theoretical account in action.
