The principle of least privilege, as articulated by Saltzer and Schroeder in their 1975 *Proceedings of the IEEE* publication, directly parallels the concept outlined in §9.5. Applied to access rights, it posits that a participant should possess only the information strictly necessary for their designated function. 

**Earlier drafts of this paper incorrectly attributed the benefit of restricted information to Simon's bounded rationality. That attribution is withdrawn.** The relevant insight stems from organizational information-processing design, not bounded rationality, which pertains to the cognitive limitations necessitating satisficing rather than deliberate informational containment.
