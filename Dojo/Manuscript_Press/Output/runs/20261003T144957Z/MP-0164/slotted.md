We employ the label **AI Sociology** to denote the specific perspective adopted in this work. Operationally, it signifies the study of represented social positions and represented social sources. This encompasses examining how attributions of an agent's place within a structure and declarations of a message's origin influence behavior, regardless of whether the described structure is explicitly implemented (§4.5.4).

This framework is distinct from the constructs delineated in §4, which possess falsification criteria. AI Sociology, as a research direction, lacks such criteria, a point elaborated upon in §12.7. Furthermore, it onderscores a specific analytical angle and should not be conflated with the established sociology of artificial intelligence, which (§2.8) focuses on AI within human society, a domain studied through existing sociological methodologies. Our utilization of the term carries no implicit claim of primacy. Rather, it serves as a shorthand for conceptual clarity within this particular analysis.
