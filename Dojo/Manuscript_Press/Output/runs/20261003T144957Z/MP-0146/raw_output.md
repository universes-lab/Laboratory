The authors explicitly claim four contributions:

1. **Reactivating historical human-AI conversations as an experimental setup.** This contribution distinguishes itself not from the mere presence of memory in agents, but from repurposing a *developed conversational relationship*—complete with role history, past disagreements, established methods, and domain-specific vocabulary—after a model version change. They assert this specific methodology hasn't been reported elsewhere. 2. **Employing conversational branch resets as a control condition (§10.5).** This methodological contribution isolates the impact of current prompts from accumulated interaction history within the same conversation. 3. **Quantitatively operationalizing unverifiable self-claims of AI agents.** While the concept of role prompts failing when asserting agent-specific truths isn't novel (§2.2), the proposed method offers a reproducible measurement (proposition count within prompts) coupled with a falsification pathway (§9.3, §11.4). 4. **Comparing operational levels of shared initial settings.** The findings are framed as measurements along a single scale, structured into a cohesive empirical program rather than isolated observations, thereby implicitly contrasting operational strata of shared contextual understanding.
