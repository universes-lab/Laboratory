Sensitivity to the ordering of in-context material is well-documented in the literature. Lu et al. (ACL 2022) demonstrate this effect, and Liu et al. (TACL 2024) further illustrate the degradation of information utilization when it resides mid-context. Notably, Sharma et al. (arXiv:2310.13548) highlight "sycophancy," where models preferentially align their responses with an interlocutor's stated beliefs, even at the expense of factual correctness. These findings directly relate to two key constructs within this paper. 

Firstly, context imprinting (§6.1) proposes a more pronounced order effect: not mere sensitivity to example arrangement, but the persistence of an interpretive stance set by a preceding conflicting interaction. Secondly, the relayed-influence observation (§5.5), where a model seemingly accommodates attributed pressure, aligns strongly with the sycophancy literature rather than functioning independently of it. Conceptualizing this as accommodation under perceived pressure situates it within the broader framework of models adapting their outputs based on perceived interlocutor biases.
