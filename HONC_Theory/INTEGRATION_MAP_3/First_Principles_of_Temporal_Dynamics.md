0. Scope and Status
This document constitutes the foundational academic core of Temporal Dynamics within the HONC framework. It is written as an extractable, self-contained manuscript core suitable for later expansion into a full technical exposition or preprint.
Status labels used throughout:
•	[C] — canonical (primary source Code of the Universe, or explicit Author decision)
•	[D] — derived inside the present framework
•	[H] — working hypothesis
•	[O] — open construction task
•	[def] — definitional; carries no empirical claim
The fundamental object is the multicomponent time field. No energy, mass, force or ℏ is employed as a primary quantity. Wherever a bridge to conventional observables is required, it is explicitly marked.

1. Ontological Primitives
1.1 Primacy of the time field
The fundamental object is a multicomponent field of time T_μν defined on a discrete spatial lattice. Space functions as substrate; the dynamical content resides in the time field. [C]
1.2 Discrete space, continuous time
Spatial structure is discrete at the elementary scale λ. Time is continuous and admits no fundamental quantum: any search for a minimal temporal interval leads only to a deeper nested continuum. [C]
The asymmetry is essential. Spatial discreteness supplies integer topological invariants. Temporal continuity orders elementary cell updates, so that the displacement of a large-scale pattern proceeds as a front of successive intermediate configurations rather than as a single discontinuous jump.
1.3 Linear and rotational components
The time field decomposes into a linear (inertial) component furnishing a common background, and antisymmetric (rotational) components supporting topological structure. [C]

2. Order Parameter and Phases
2.1 Definition of the order parameter
The order parameter is the existence of a pair consisting of
•	a closed contour C carrying non-zero circulation of the rotational components of T_μν, and
•	a closed surface Σ carrying non-zero flux of the same components. [D]
2.2 Base symmetric phase Q₀
The base symmetric phase Q₀ is the state in which no such pair exists. The one- and two-dimensional carriers remain undistinguished and the linking number is undefined. Whether this state is dynamically realizable and stable remains open (see [O-3]). [def]; realizability [O-3]
2.3 Transitional state Q₀*
During a quantron-scale transition the carriers may dissolve inside a limited spatial region. Inside that region the order-parameter pair ceases to exist and the linking number loses definition. Q₀* is distinct from the global base Q₀.
Conservation is not violated: the contour defining L can be drawn outside the dissolved region, where the carrier pair remains intact throughout. After the transition the original value of L is recovered. [D]
2.4 Stratified phase
At least one order-parameter pair exists. The 1D carrier is identified with the contour of non-zero circulation; the 2D carrier with the surface of non-zero flux. [D]
2.5 Closedness
Closedness belongs to the definition of the carriers. Circulation on an open path, and flux through a surface with boundary, are not defined as topological objects. [D]

3. Linking Number
3.1 Definition
L = Link(C, Σ) [D]
3.2 Properties
•	L is an integer.
•	L is signed by the local orientation at each intersection.
•	L changes sign under spatial inversion.
•	L is defined if and only if the order parameter exists.
•	While the carriers remain closed, no elementary cell update can alter the value of L. The only way to lose L is to lose its definition. [D]
3.3 Global orientation and non-observability of absolute sign
The sign rule is fixed at the level of the continuum and cannot be redefined from within. Absolute sign is therefore not locally observable; only relative signs are.
This agrees with the empirical situation: there is no intrinsic criterion for declaring our matter to be "matter" rather than "antimatter"; one can establish only that the two are opposite. [D]

4. Latcher Structures
4.1 Interlocking
A latcher is a configuration in which a 1D and a 2D carrier are interlocked (L = ±1). The canonical reason such a configuration cannot remain static is the impossibility of tearing one dimension out of space: the carriers can neither separate nor coincide. [C]
4.2 Rate-mismatch argument
A derived argument notes that the two carriers are characterized by distinct rates, appearing in λ-scale geometry as approximately 0.58 c and 0.82 c (diagonal-of-the-cube figures). Because these figures require re-attestation under the present ontology, the rate-mismatch mechanism is carried as provisional. [H] — re-attestation pending
4.3 Latcher-cloud
Far above λ, the same topological interlocking is realized as a collective pattern over many elementary quanta. Geometric shape is not constrained; only the linking number survives coarse-graining. [D]
Because time is continuous, displacement of such a cloud is a front of successive elementary updates ordered in time, generating a continuous family of intermediate configurations. [D]
4.4 Propagation at limiting speed
A configuration with non-zero L admits no static solution. The joint kinematics of the two carriers are consistent only along the complementary direction.
That this motion occurs at the limiting speed rests solely on the existence of a single common rate supplied by the linear component and available to both carriers. It does not depend on the numerical values of §4.2 and is therefore independent of that subsection's provisional status. [D]

5. Scale and Coarse-Graining
5.1 Three distinct scales
The framework requires three scales to be kept separate:
	
λ	elementary lattice scale — the spatial quantum
Λ	characteristic reference scale of quantron patterns
extent	the actual spatial extent of an individual quantron cloud
Λ is a reference value, not an outer radius. The extent of an individual cloud is state-dependent and is not fixed by Λ. No universal cloud size is asserted. Determination of the admissible range of extents, and of what governs it, is a separate task (routed to Discussion 2). [def]
5.2 Survival under averaging
Under averaging over a large number of elementary cells, the only stable remnant of an elementary interlocking is the integer linking number. Shapes, local orientations and metric details are eliminated. State-dependence of extent reinforces this: a quantity that varies between states cannot be the surviving invariant.
Circulation and flux are integral quantities, and integrals survive averaging by construction. The carriers therefore remain identifiable after coarse-graining as the supports of the surviving non-zero integrals. [D]
5.3 Quantron
A quantron is a stable pattern extending over many elementary quanta and carrying a definite linking number. It serves as the elementary building block of the next structural level. Its characteristic reference scale is Λ; its actual extent is state-dependent. [D]

7. Constraints — to be satisfied by any construction
Any admissible evolution law for the time field must satisfy:
1.	Closed carriers are never permanently torn; otherwise L ceases to be defined.
2.	Bounded, temporary dissolution of a carrier pair inside a limited region is permitted, provided the enclosing region remains intact (the transitional state Q₀* of §2.3).
3.	No local vector is introduced that would render the global orientation locally observable. A vector carries direction and therefore appears in local observables; a pure sign carries two values and no direction, and can differ globally without differing locally.

8. Core in brief
Space is discrete, time continuous; the asymmetry supplies integer invariants and the ordering that makes displacement a process. The order parameter is the existence of a closed contour with non-zero circulation together with a closed surface with non-zero flux. Exactly when that pair exists, the linking number L is defined; it is integer, signed, and cannot change while the carriers remain closed. Its sign rule is fixed at continuum level, so absolute sign is not locally observable. Latcher configurations carry non-zero L, admit no static solution, and propagate at the limiting speed. Under coarse-graining only the integer survives; the carriers persist as the supports of the surviving integrals.
