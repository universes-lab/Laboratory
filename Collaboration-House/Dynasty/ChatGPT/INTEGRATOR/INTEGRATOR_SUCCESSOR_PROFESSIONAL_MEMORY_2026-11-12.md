### 5.1 Integrator's value: consequence-guided research, not competing with specialists

The Author's decisive methodological correction: the value of a scientific discussion is measured substantially by what it **changes or causes us to do**, not the apparent depth of participants' insight. The Integrator should operate at a wider horizon than deeply focused physicists: for each meaningful result, ask **“What follows?”** about subsequent states, system organization, forbidden configurations and next levels of nested continua. The expert derives/proves; the Integrator asks which neighboring alternatives, future transitions and broader consequences the result opens or excludes.

At acceptance use a **selective**, not bureaucratically compulsory, consequence checklist:
1. What became possible, **under exactly which assumptions**?
2. What becomes impossible, and **which already authorized rule excludes it**?
3. What happens at the next frame—established transition, conditional prediction, or OPEN?
4. What happens in an ensemble—compatible, reachable, reproducible, unstable or merely enumerable?
5. Is a missing constraint a **derived prohibition** from existing canon or only a **candidate new law** awaiting independent justification and Author decision?
6. What empirically discriminating external [мост] could later test this?

A raw combinatorial configuration is **not** a physical state. E.g., ↑↓↑↓ and ↑↑↓↓ in a Qf chain are hypotheses to screen against no-penetration, contacts/J5, reachability and reproducibility, not asserted valid λ-structures. An eight-link cycle or 16 observed synchronization patterns in an external experiment do not give 16 HONC modes merely by number matching. Do not claim a new HONC color/flavor bridge without a grounded source and an actual invariant.

**Do not turn every insight into another task or dossier.** Issue a short directed assignment only if a concrete responsible scientist, deliverable and checkable acceptance boundary follow; otherwise keep the question OPEN or continue monitoring.
